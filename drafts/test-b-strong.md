# Your AI Pilot Didn't Fail. It Died in Week Two.

### Everyone measures launch-day adoption. The number that predicts whether a tool survives is the one nobody pulls.

**TL;DR:** Week-one usage measures curiosity. Week-two usage measures value. If your retention from week one to week two is under 40%, the tool is already dead and your dashboard hasn't noticed. Pull that number today — it takes ten minutes.

---

A regional insurance brokerage rolled out an AI claims summarizer to 240 adjusters in March. Launch week: 187 users, 78% adoption. The VP sent a celebratory note to the board.

I pulled the same report in week six. Active users: 31.

Nobody had lied. The launch number was real. It was just measuring the wrong thing.

**Week one measures curiosity. Week two measures value.**

Everyone logs in the first week. Their manager announced it, there's a training session on the calendar, and there's novelty in a new tool. None of that is a signal. You are measuring compliance and mild interest, and both decay.

Week two is when the calendar invite is gone and nobody is watching. A person who opens the tool in week two opens it because it made their Tuesday easier. That is the first honest number you get.

At the brokerage, week-one-to-week-two retention was 23%. The tool was dead in March. They found out in May, after two more months of licensing fees and a scheduled expansion to a second business unit.

**The threshold that matters**

Across the deployments I have reviewed, the pattern is consistent enough to plan around:

- **Under 40%** — the tool is dead. Stop the expansion. Something about the workflow fit is broken, and more training will not fix it.
- **40-60%** — it works for a subset. Find out which subset and why, then decide whether that segment justifies the contract.
- **Over 60%** — you have something. Now expand.

The brokerage's 23% was not a training problem, which is what they spent April assuming. Adjusters told me the summarizer was genuinely good at summarizing — but it dropped the adjuster's own prior notes from the summary, so every user had to re-open the original file anyway. The tool saved four minutes and cost five.

That is a workflow problem, and it was visible in the week-two number sixty days before anyone diagnosed it.

**What to do this week**

Pull week-one-to-week-two retention for every AI tool you have deployed in the last year. Most vendor dashboards will not show it directly — you want the count of users active in days 8-14 divided by the count active in days 1-7.

If you cannot get that number from your vendor, that is itself the finding. Ask them why a usage metric that basic is not in the product.

Then, for anything under 40%, go ask five users what they do *instead*. Not whether they like the tool. What they actually do when the work lands on their desk. The answer is usually a workflow detail nobody asked about before procurement signed.
