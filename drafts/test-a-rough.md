# Some Thoughts On AI Agents

In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape, artificial intelligence continues to transform how organizations operate. It's an exciting time to be working in this space.

Everyone is talking about agents right now. If you've been following the industry, you've probably seen the term everywhere. It seems like every vendor has repositioned around it.

Before we go further, it's worth defining our terms. An agent, broadly speaking, is a system that can take actions autonomously toward a goal. This is distinct from a chatbot, which simply responds to prompts. Most definitions agree on this basic framing, though there is some disagreement at the margins.

The technology has matured considerably over the past eighteen months. Model capabilities have improved, tooling has become more robust, and the ecosystem has expanded. Most companies are seeing real gains from these deployments, and adoption continues to accelerate across industries.

So what does this mean for leaders? There are a number of considerations to keep in mind as you evaluate whether to leverage this paradigm shift within your own organization.

But here's what most teams get wrong: they deploy an agent to a process nobody has ever written down.

I watched this happen at a dental practice in Ohio last spring. They bought an AI scheduling agent, wired it to their calendar, and turned it on. Within a week it had double-booked eleven patients and cancelled a root canal. Not because the model was bad — because the front desk had three unwritten rules about which appointment types could overlap, and Denise, who had worked there for nineteen years, was the only one who knew them. Nobody had ever asked her. The agent did exactly what it was told. What it was told was incomplete.

The lesson isn't that agents don't work. It's that an agent is a mirror. Point it at a process you understand and it scales that understanding. Point it at a process that lives in one person's head and it scales the gaps instead.

So before you deploy anything, go find your Denise. Ask her what she knows that isn't written down anywhere. That conversation is the actual implementation work. The software is the easy part.

Anyway, those are my thoughts. Let me know what you think.
